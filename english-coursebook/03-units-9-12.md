### Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, you will be able to:

- Talk about imaginary situations and their results using the second conditional.
- Discuss environmental issues and ways to protect the planet.
- Give advice using "If I were you..."

### Grammar: Second Conditional

**Form**

We use the second conditional to talk about imaginary or unreal situations in the present or future.

> **If + past simple, would + base verb**

| If clause (condition) | Main clause (result) |
|-----------------------|----------------------|
| If I had more money, | I would buy an electric car. |
| If everyone recycled, | there would be less waste. |
| If we lived near the beach, | we would swim every day. |

**Use**

We use the second conditional for:

1. **Imaginary situations** — things that are not true or unlikely to happen.
   - *If I were the president, I would stop pollution.* (I am not the president.)
   - *If we had a garden, we would grow our own vegetables.* (We don't have a garden.)

2. **Giving advice** — "If I were you..."
   - *If I were you, I would start recycling.*
   - *If I were you, I would use less plastic.*

**First Conditional vs. Second Conditional**

| First Conditional (real/possible) | Second Conditional (unreal/imaginary) |
|------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| If it rains, I will take an umbrella. (possible) | If it rained, I would stay home. (unlikely/imaginary) |
| If you study, you will pass. (real possibility) | If I were a bird, I would fly. (imaginary) |
| If I have time, I will help you. (real) | If I had a million dollars, I would travel the world. (imaginary) |

> **Common Mistakes**
>
> ❌ *If I would have more time, I would exercise.*
> ✅ *If I had more time, I would exercise.*
>
> Do NOT use "would" in the if-clause. Use the past simple in the if-clause.

> ❌ *If I was you, I would recycle more.*
> ✅ *If I were you, I would recycle more.*
>
> Use "were" (not "was") after "If I..." in the second conditional.

### Reading

#### What Would You Do to Save the Planet?

Most of us know that our planet is in trouble. Climate change is causing temperatures to rise, and pollution is damaging our air and water. But what would you do if you had to make big changes to your lifestyle?

Imagine if everyone in your city decided to recycle more. The amount of waste going to landfills would decrease by half. What if all your neighbours started using renewable energy? The air would become cleaner, and your city would be healthier.

Some people say they would do more for the environment if they had more money or time. But small changes can make a big difference. If you carried a reusable water bottle, you would reduce plastic waste. If you walked or cycled for short trips, you would reduce your carbon footprint. If you bought less food and ate what you had, less food would go to waste.

Of course, we cannot solve the problem alone. But imagine what would happen if everyone made just one small change. If one million people stopped using plastic bags, there would be millions fewer bags in the ocean. If every household turned off lights when leaving a room, we would save huge amounts of energy.

If I were you, I would start with one small change today. What would you choose?

#### Listening Script: Office Recycling

**Sarah:** Hey Mark, have you noticed how much paper we throw away in this office?

**Mark:** I know. It's terrible. If we had a better recycling system, we would waste less.

**Sarah:** Exactly. If I were the manager, I would put recycling bins next to every desk.

**Mark:** That's a great idea. And if people could see how much waste they produce, they would probably change their habits.

**Sarah:** True. Also, if we used less printing paper, we would save a lot of trees.

**Mark:** I agree. I try to read documents on screen, but sometimes I need to print.

**Mark:** Those are good ideas. Maybe we should talk to the manager about this.

**Sarah:** Definitely. If we suggest these changes together, she might listen.

*(The conversation continues as they plan to speak to their manager.)*

### Grammar: Relative Clauses

**Defining Relative Clauses**

We use defining relative clauses to give essential information about a person, thing, or place. Without this information, the sentence would not be complete.

| Pronoun | Used for | Example |
|---------|----------|---------|
| **who** | people | The woman **who lives next door** is a doctor. |
| **which** | things and animals | The book **which is on the table** is mine. |
| **that** | people and things | The man **that called you** is my brother. / The car **that is parked outside** is new. |
| **where** | places | The restaurant **where we met** is now closed. |

**Omission of the Relative Pronoun**

When the relative pronoun is the **object** of the clause (not the subject), we can omit it.

- *The man **who** I met yesterday was very kind.* → *The man I met yesterday was very kind.*
- *The film **which** we watched was great.* → *The film we watched was great.*
- But we CANNOT omit the pronoun when it is the **subject**:
  - *The woman **who** lives next door is kind.* (cannot omit "who")

> **Common Mistakes**
>
> ❌ *The person which called you is here.*
> ✅ *The person who called you is here.*
>
> Use "who" (not "which") for people.

> ❌ *That's the house who I grew up in.*
> ✅ *That's the house where I grew up.* / *That's the house which I grew up in.*
>
> Use "where" for places.

### Grammar: Passive Voice

**Present Simple Passive**

> **am / is / are + past participle**

| Active | Passive |
|--------|---------|
| The journalist writes the article. | The article **is written** by the journalist. |
| People speak English here. | English **is spoken** here. |
| The police arrest the suspect. | The suspect **is arrested** by the police. |

**Past Simple Passive**

> **was / were + past participle**

| Active | Passive |
|--------|---------|
| The reporter wrote the story. | The story **was written** by the reporter. |
| Someone stole my bag. | My bag **was stolen**. |
| The police arrested the thief. | The thief **was arrested** by the police. |

**When to Use the Passive Voice**

We use the passive voice when:

1. **The agent (doer) is unknown.**
   - *My phone was stolen.* (We don't know who stole it.)

2. **The agent is obvious or not important.**
   - *The suspect was arrested last night.* (It is obvious the police did it.)

3. **We want to focus on the action or the result, not the person.**
   - *The new hospital was opened in March.* (The hospital is more important than who opened it.)

**By + Agent**

If we want to mention who did the action, we use **by**:

- *The article **was written** by a famous journalist.*
- *The window **was broken** by the storm.*

> **Common Mistakes**
>
> ❌ *The window was broke.*
> ✅ *The window was broken.*
>
> Use the past participle, not the past simple.

> ❌ *The news is wrote by journalists.*
> ✅ *The news is written by journalists.*
>
> Irregular past participles must be learned.

> ❌ *The man was arrested from the police.*
> ✅ *The man was arrested by the police.*
>
> Use "by" (not "from") to mention the agent.

#### A Year of Changes

Looking back, I can hardly believe how much my life has changed in the past twelve months. Last January, I was unhappy with my daily routine. I was waking up late, eating unhealthy food, and spending too much time on social media. I knew something had to change, but I had tried before and failed. My first goal was to improve my health. I started running every morning. At first, I could only run for five minutes without stopping, but I did not give up. By the end of the first month, I was running for thirty minutes. I also changed my diet. I reduced the amount of sugar I ate and started cooking more meals at home. After six months, I had lost ten kilograms and felt more energetic than ever. If someone had told me a year ago that I would enjoy running, I would not have believed them.

Next, I focused on my career. I had been working in the same job for five years and felt stuck. I was not learning anything new, and I was not happy. I decided to take an online course in digital marketing, which was something I had always been interested in. It was difficult because I was studying after work, but I was determined. After three months, I completed the course and applied for a new position. I got the job! My new role is challenging but rewarding, and I work with people who inspire me. The company I work for now treats its employees well, and good work is always recognized.

My personal life also improved. I started calling my family more often and made an effort to meet new people. I joined a local book club, where I met my best friend, Anna. We share the same taste in books and have great conversations about everything. She is the kind of person who makes you feel better about life.

Of course, not everything was easy. There were days when I wanted to give up, and moments when I felt like nothing was changing. But I learned that progress is not always fast. Small steps, taken every day, can lead to big results. If you are reading this and thinking about making a change, I encourage you to start. You do not have to change everything at once. Just pick one thing and begin.

This year has taught me that change is possible if you are willing to work for it. A year from now, you will be glad you started today.

### Pronunciation: Review

This unit reviews key pronunciation features from the course.

**1. Sentence Stress**

In English sentences, content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives) are stressed, and grammar words (prepositions, articles) are unstressed.

- *I **met** my **friend** at the **park** **yesterday**.*
- *She **studies** **English** **every** **day**.*

**2. Weak Forms**

Many common words have weak forms when they are not stressed:
- *I can /kən/ swim.* (not /kæn/)
- *I would /wəd/ help.* (not /wʊd/)
- *There is /ðəz/ a problem.* (not /ðiː ɪz/)

**3. Contractions**

Contractions are very common in spoken English:
- *I am* → **I'm**
- *I have* → **I've**
- *I would / I had* → **I'd**
- *She is / She has* → **She's**
- *Do not* → **Don't**
- *Will not* → **Won't**
